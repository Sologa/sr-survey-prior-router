Checklist for initial submissions  
Your manuscript will be subject to a quality check before it can be presented to an Editorial Board 
Member (EBM) for assessment. This is to ensure that all information required by the EBM and peer-
reviewers is available.  
Please review the checklist before submitting, as manuscripts that do not comply may be returned 
for changes before they are assigned to an EBM. Additionally, please ensure that all author 
information entered both in your manuscript and on our system is accurate.  
Should you have any questions regarding this checklist, please contact us at 
scientificreviews@nature.com

MAIN TEXT 
 
For the main body of the text, there are no specific requirements. You can organize it in a way that 
best suits the goal of your review and the type of narrative review you plan to submit. However, 
the following structure will be suitable in many cases:  
• 
Introduction - provides background, context, and rationale for the review, highlighting the 
importance of the topic and specific points or arguments being raised.  
• 
Main body - laid out in themed subsections organized by topic, methodology, or trend 
instead of chronologically. This section should:  
• 
Synthesize and critically appraise - compare findings, highlight successes, 
failures, and inconsistencies, rather than simply aggregating findings 
• 
Identify Knowledge Gaps - emphasize what is missing or unknown areas in the 
field 
• 
Conclusion - Summarizes key points, reflects on the significance of the findings, and 
proposes future research directions 
 
No footnotes

AUTHOR CONTRIBUTIONS STATEMENT 
 
A detailed author contributions statement is included after the references, specifying 
the specific intellectual roles undertaken by each named author (for example: conceptual 
development, analytical framework, literature synthesis, critical interpretation, drafting, and 
revision). We recommend denoting authors using their initials 
☐ 
The contributions statement clearly articulates how analytical judgment and interpretative 
responsibility were shared across authors, consistent with the narrative review format. Where 
responsibilities were shared (e.g. conceptualization, synthesis, interpretation), this sharing is 
explicitly stated rather than implied 
☐ 
Every named author has a corresponding contribution description; no authors are listed without 
a defined role 
☐ 
The contributions statement explicitly states that the Lead Author provided conceptual 
leadership during manuscript preparation and served as a source of accountability for the review. 
If more than one Lead Author is named, their respective leadership roles and areas of 
responsibility are clearly distinguished 
☐ 
A short author biography is included for each named author.  
Each biography states: 
• 
The author’s current role, institutional affiliation, and career stage (where relevant) 
• 
Clearly describe each author’s subject-matter expertise and relevance to the review 
topic

ADDITIONAL INFORMATION  
 
A Competing interests statement is provided in the Manuscript File under the heading “Additional 
Information”, and matches the information entered on the submission system. Competing 
financial and non-financial interests should be disclosed 
☐ 
LEGENDS 
 
A legend is provided for each main table and main figure in the Manuscript File. These should be 
placed at the end of the manuscript, after the references 
☐ 
For supplementary figures and tables, include a brief title and legend (incorporated into the file to 
appear near the image) 
☐ 
Only legends for main display items should be included in the Manuscript File; legends for 
supplementary figures and tables should be included in the Supplementary Information file 
☐ 
REFERENCES 
 
In a numbered list and all in-line citations should be matched back to a reference via numbering 
within square brackets 
☐ 
All references provided in the manuscript text. If you are making your submission by LaTeX, it 
must either contain all references within the manuscript .tex file itself, or (if you’re using the 
Overleaf template) include the .bbl file generated during the compilation process as a ‘LaTeX 
supplementary file’. BibTeX (.bib) bibliography files for references will not be accepted 
☐
